## Summary

DeepSeek, once known for nearly free AI, has launched its V4 Pro model at prices up to 14 times higher than its budget offerings, charging $1.32/$3.96 per million tokens. This premium pricing, despite V4 Pro's marginal performance improvement over its cheaper V4 Flash, contradicts DeepSeek's prior narrative that AI was becoming too inexpensive. The company, which once caused a massive market dip for Nvidia by suggesting AI infrastructure was unnecessary, is now raising billions for data centers and chip development. This shift reveals that frontier AI remains costly to build and serve, forcing companies without cloud platforms to reflect true compute expenses in their pricing. DeepSeek's actions now validate the significant investment required for advanced AI.

DeepSeek spent the last eighteen months as the market's favorite proof that AI was getting too cheap to justify the money being spent on it. Last Friday the company released V4 Pro, its new flagship, at [$1.32 per million input tokens and $3.96 per million output tokens](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/deepseek-launches-v4-pro-at-prices-up-to-14-times-higher-than-v4-flash-126081401577_1.html). Its budget V4 Flash model costs $0.14 and $0.28. The company that made its name on nearly free intelligence is now charging up to fourteen times its own floor price for its best model.

The lab that taught investors to fear cheap AI is now selling intelligence at a premium, and its own price list has become the strongest argument against the story it started.

## Fourteen Times The Price

V4 Pro is aimed at the expensive end of the market. DeepSeek says the model substantially improves agent capabilities, the tasks where software plans and executes work on its own, and it ships through the company's API, app, and web products as the new flagship.

What buyers get for the premium is narrower than the price jump suggests. On the [Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2088440350734201149), an independent composite of ten benchmarks, V4 Pro scores 53. **That is a single point above DeepSeek's own near-free V4 Flash at 52, and ten points below Claude Opus 5 at 63, the top of the board.** Per unit of work, DeepSeek remains inexpensive: about [$0.06 per completed benchmark task](https://officechai.com/ai/deepseek-v4-0813-pro-benchmarks/), against $2.34 for Claude Opus 5. This launch does not put DeepSeek anywhere near Western price levels.

DeepSeek's own catalog now says that better intelligence costs multiples more to buy, which is precisely the economics its existence was supposed to have broken. A company built on the claim that frontier capability could be nearly free has concluded, with its own margins on the line, that it cannot be.

## The $589 Billion Memory

The reason this pricing decision matters to equity investors is January 27, 2025. DeepSeek's R1 reasoning model, released with a claim that training cost less than $6 million, convinced the market that the hundreds of billions being poured into AI infrastructure might be unnecessary. Nvidia fell 17 percent that day and lost [$589 billion in market capitalization](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-stock-plummets-loses-record-589-billion-as-deepseek-prompts-questions-over-ai-spending-135105824.html), the largest one-day loss in stock market history at the time. No cost estimate in tech history had moved so much money so fast.

The correction arrived within weeks. SemiAnalysis reported that DeepSeek operated a fleet of [roughly 50,000 Nvidia Hopper GPUs and had spent about $1.6 billion](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/deepseek-might-not-be-as-disruptive-as-claimed-firm-reportedly-has-50-000-nvidia-gpus-and-spent-usd1-6-billion-on-buildouts) on servers, with the $6 million figure covering only the final training run. The legend outran the facts anyway. Nvidia was back at [record highs by June 25, 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-stock-notches-fresh-record-high-in-remarkable-2025-turnaround-200401502.html), yet every efficiency headline since has been read through the January template, including the scare the market relived this July around Moonshot AI's Kimi K3. The market corrected the number and kept trading the myth.

Our letter to subscribers that same day said: **"Don't listen to them. Bears are missing the bigger picture."** It named the weakness as an accumulation opportunity in Nvidia, Broadcom, and Arista Networks. It also made an argument that looked abstract at the time: models would be commoditized, and the value would rest with the platform, not the model.

## Two Price Lists From China

A year and a half later, that argument is playing out inside China's own AI market, in two price lists published three weeks apart.

Alibaba [priced its 2.4 trillion parameter flagship at $2 per million input tokens](https://paste-qwen-piece-url-here/) in early August, cheaper than the mid-tier products from OpenAI and Anthropic. It can afford to, because the model is a funnel for the cloud computing Alibaba actually charges for. The token price does not need to carry the business.

DeepSeek has no cloud underneath it. It sells tokens, and serving reasoning-heavy workloads consumes real compute on real GPUs drawing real power. When that is the whole business, the price list has to tell the truth about costs. Moonshot learned the same lesson in July, when Kimi K3’s popularity forced it to [pause new signups](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/chinas-moonshot-pauses-kimi-subscriptions-080317250.html) and ration access to the compute it had. Our July letter, written while that scare was unfolding, put the pattern plainly:

_It is not different this time. Important technological advances lead to more growth, not less._

A premium tier at DeepSeek and a giveaway tier at Alibaba are the same fact wearing different clothes. Intelligence at the commodity level keeps getting cheaper, exactly as it should. Intelligence near the frontier stays scarce and expensive to serve, and whoever lacks a platform to subsidize it has to charge for it.

## The Buildout Behind The Prices

DeepSeek's spending confirms what its pricing implies. The company closed its first outside funding round in June at about $7.4 billion. This month it [resumed a second round seeking close to $8 billion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/deepseek-resumes-8-billion-round-with-monolith-in-the-running) at a valuation near $74 billion, while [preparing a listing on Shanghai's STAR Market](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/15/deepseek-eyes-74-billion-valuation-in-new-funding-round-ahead-of-planned-ipo/). It plans to roughly double its workforce, with the growth concentrated in data-center and AI-agent teams. It is also developing its own chips to reduce dependence on outside suppliers.

None of this is the behavior of a company that broke the economics of compute. **A company whose efficiency supposedly made AI infrastructure unnecessary is raising billions of dollars to buy AI infrastructure.** The demand is so far ahead of supply that the world's most famous efficiency lab is building data centers and designing silicon. It is charging a premium to manage access to what it already has.

## The Exhibit Switched Sides

Another efficiency scare will arrive on schedule. Some lab will claim frontier quality at a fraction of frontier cost, the chip complex will wobble, and the January 2025 template will get another run.

What last Friday's price list adds is the ending of the original story. The commodity tier keeps getting cheaper, which widens what AI gets used for. Frontier capability still costs real money to build and to serve, and DeepSeek has now said so in the only language markets fully trust, its own price list. Nvidia, which surrendered $589 billion in a day to DeepSeek's legend, is the company DeepSeek now depends on and emulates.

**The strongest exhibit the bears ever had is now spending like the bulls.**
