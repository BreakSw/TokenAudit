**Grok** is a series of [generative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_AI "Generative AI") [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model") developed by [SpaceXAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceXAI "SpaceXAI"). It was launched in November 2023 by [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") as an initiative based on the [large language model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model") (LLM) of the same name. Grok has apps for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS") and [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_(operating_system) "Android (operating system)") and is integrated with the [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network) "X (social network)") social network and [Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla,_Inc. "Tesla, Inc.")'s [Optimus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimus_(robot) "Optimus (robot)") robot where it has [chatbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatbot "Chatbot") functionality. Grok is named after the verb _[to grok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok "Grok")_, created by the American [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") author [Robert A. Heinlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Heinlein "Robert A. Heinlein") to convey a form of deep, intuitive understanding.

SpaceXAI has released several Grok models. Grok-1 was open-sourced in 2024,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-3) followed by Grok-2, Grok 3, and Grok 4 model families,[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-4) with Grok 4.5 released in 2026.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:10-5) Different versions added capabilities including image generation,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-6) web search,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-7) "Think" mode to simulate reasoning,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-8), agentic coding tool called Grok Build,[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-9) and AI teammates called Grok Bot.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-10) Musk admitted in 2026 that Grok was partially [distilled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_distillation "Knowledge distillation") from [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI")'s GPT models.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-12) Starting with Grok 4.5, the models are co-developed with incoming SpaceXAI subsidiary [Cursor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursor_(company) "Cursor (company)").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:10-5)

The bot has received criticism for promoting [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theories "Conspiracy theories"), praising [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler"), using [antisemitic tropes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitic_tropes "Antisemitic tropes"), and [creating nonconsensual, sexualized images of undressed women and children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_sexual_deepfake_scandal "Grok sexual deepfake scandal").

Grok generates articles for the [encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia "Encyclopedia") website [Grokipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grokipedia "Grokipedia"), launched in October 2025 by Musk as an alternative to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia "Wikipedia"), which he described as " [woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke "Woke")" and [politically biased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideological_bias_on_Wikipedia "Ideological bias on Wikipedia").

## Background

### OpenAI

Musk was one of the 11 co-founders of [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI"), and initially co-chaired it with [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-13) He left the company's board in 2018, saying of his decision that he "didn't agree with some of what OpenAI team wanted to do".[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-bi-14) OpenAI went on to launch [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT") in the end of 2022, and [GPT-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-4 "GPT-4") in March 2023. The same month, Musk was one of the individuals to sign the " [Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pause_Giant_AI_Experiments:_An_Open_Letter "Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter")" from the Future of Life Institute, which called for a six-month pause in the development of any AI software more powerful than GPT-4.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-15)

### TruthGPT

In April 2023, Musk said in an interview on _[Tucker Carlson Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson_Tonight "Tucker Carlson Tonight")_ that he intended to develop an AI chatbot called "TruthGPT", which he described as "a maximum truth-seeking AI that tries to understand the nature of the universe".[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-bi-14) He expressed concern to [Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson "Tucker Carlson") that ChatGPT was being "trained to be [politically correct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politically_correct "Politically correct")".[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-16)

### Grok

TruthGPT would later be renamed after _[grok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok "Grok")_, a verb coined by American author [Robert A. Heinlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Heinlein "Robert A. Heinlein") in his 1961 science fiction novel _[Stranger in a Strange Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranger_in_a_Strange_Land "Stranger in a Strange Land")_ to describe a deeper than human form of understanding.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-SpicyGrok-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-18)

In November 2023, [xAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XAI_(company) "XAI (company)") began previewing Grok as a chatbot to select users,[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-19) with participation in the early access program being limited to [paid X Premium users](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(social_network)#Premium "X (social network)").[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-20) It was announced that once the bot was out of [early beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_release_life_cycle#Beta "Software release life cycle"), it would only be available to higher tier X Premium subscribers.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-21) At the time of the preview, xAI described the chatbot as "a very early beta product – the best we could do with 2 months of training" that could "improve rapidly with each passing week".[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-xai-2023-22)

On March 11, 2024, Musk posted on X that the language model would go open source within a week. Six days later, on March 17, Grok-1 was [open sourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software "Open-source software") under the [Apache-2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_License "Apache License") license.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-verge-2024-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-24) Disclosed were the network's architecture and its weight parameters.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-IEEE-25) On March 26, 2024, Musk announced that Grok would be enabled for X Premium subscribers, in addition to those on Premium+.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-26)

On March 29, 2024, Grok-1.5 was announced, with "improved reasoning capabilities" and a context length of 128,000 tokens.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-xai-2024a-27) Grok-1.5 was released to all X Premium users on May 15, 2024.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-xai-about-1) On April 4, 2024, an update to X's "Explore" page included summaries of breaking news stories written by Grok, a task previously assigned to a human curation team.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-mashable-28)

On April 12, 2024, Grok-1.5 Vision (Grok-1.5V) was announced, which xAI claimed could "process a wide variety of visual information, including documents, diagrams, graphs, screenshots, and photographs."[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-xai-grok-1.5v-29) However, Grok-1.5V was never released to the public. On May 4, 2024, Grok became available in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"),[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-30) which was the only European country to support it at the time due to the impending [Artificial Intelligence Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Act "Artificial Intelligence Act") rules in the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"). Grok was later reviewed by the EU and was released on May 16, 2024.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-31)

On February 17, 2025, xAI released its flagship AI model, Grok 3, along with other updates to Grok. [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") stated that Grok 3 was trained with "10x" more computing power than its predecessor, Grok-2, utilizing the data center [Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_(supercomputer) "Colossus (supercomputer)"), containing around 200,000 GPUs.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-51) The model was trained on an expanded dataset that reportedly included legal filings, with xAI claiming it outperformed [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI")'s [GPT-4o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-4o "GPT-4o") on benchmarks such as [AIME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Invitational_Mathematics_Examination "American Invitational Mathematics Examination") for mathematical reasoning and GPQA for PhD-level science problems.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-52) xAI also released Grok 3 mini, which offered faster responses at the cost of some accuracy.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch-2025-53) Additionally, xAI introduced [reasoning capabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning_language_model "Reasoning language model") similar to reasoning models like OpenAI's [o3-mini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O3-mini "O3-mini") and [DeepSeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeepSeek "DeepSeek")'s R1, allowing users to access a Think mode to enable reasoning or a Big Brain mode for complex problem-solving, which utilized more computing resources.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch-2025-53) The 'Big Brain' mode was never made publicly available.

xAI claimed that Grok 3 Reasoning surpassed the best version of OpenAI's o3-mini, o3-mini-high, on several popular benchmarks, including a newer mathematics benchmark called AIME 2025[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch-2025-53) based on problems from the [American Invitational Mathematics Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Invitational_Mathematics_Examination "American Invitational Mathematics Examination") (AIME) 2025 exam.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-54) An OpenAI employee criticized xAI's published comparison graph, pointing out that it included the Grok 3 results using the "consensus@64" technique (making 64 runs and selecting the most frequent answer), and only showed the o3-mini-high results without this technique.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-55) xAI also introduced DeepSearch, a feature that scanned the internet and X to generate detailed summaries in response to queries, positioning it as a competitor to OpenAI's [ChatGPT Deep Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT_Deep_Research "ChatGPT Deep Research").[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch-2025-53)

Initially, access to Grok 3 was limited to X's Premium+ and xAI's SuperGrok subscribers, with plans to offer it later via xAI's enterprise API. Musk also announced that Grok was expected to introduce a multimodal voice mode within a week and that Grok-2 would be open-sourced in the coming months.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch-2025-53) Hours after the announcement, X raised the price of its Premium+ subscription to $40.00 per month, up from $22.00.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-56) Grok 3 was made available to free users on February 20, 2025, for a "short time".[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-57) This access was never disabled, despite being initially described as temporary. On February 22, 2025, xAI updated the Grok logo yet again, featuring a [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole "Black hole") and a new tagline "To understand".[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-58)

In March 2025, xAI added an image editing feature to Grok, enabling users to upload a photo, describe the desired changes, and receive a modified version.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechRadar-2025-59) Alongside this, xAI released DeeperSearch, an enhanced version of DeepSearch that utilizes extended search and more reasoning.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Decoder-2025-60) In April 2025, xAI launched an API for Grok 3. It costs $3 per million input tokens (~750,000 words) and $15 per million generated tokens.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-61) In May 2025, Grok 3 was announced for [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure "Microsoft Azure").[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-62) In July 2025 Musk announced that Grok had been "significantly improved"[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-The_Telegraph-63) and noted that users would "notice a difference".[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:0-64) When the chatbot was found to be posting antisemitic content and praising Hitler, days later, some of these changes were reversed.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-grokoffline-65)

### Grok 4 Fast

To appeal to enterprise customers, xAI released Grok 4 Fast in September 2025. Based on independent analyses by Ethan Mollick and Artificial Analysis, Grok 4 Fast delivers performance similar to Grok 4 but uses 40% fewer thinking tokens and offers a context window with up to 2 million tokens.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:1-70) Grok 4 Fast is also up to 64× cheaper than early frontier models like OpenAI's o3, making xAI's offerings more accessible and potentially accelerating adoption.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:1-70)

### Grok Code Fast 1

On August 28, 2025, xAI released Grok Code Fast 1,[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-grokfast1-71) a speedy and economical reasoning model that excels at agentic coding. ### Grok 4.1

On November 17, 2025, xAI released **Grok 4.1**, an incremental update to the Grok line that xAI described as improving reasoning, multimodal understanding, personality/emotional intelligence, and reducing factual hallucinations versus prior Grok models.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Grok4.1-72) The release followed a two-week silent rollout (1–14 November 2025) during which xAI reported blind pairwise evaluations on live traffic and used feedback to refine the model's behavior.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Grok4.1-72)

xAI simultaneously announced **Grok 4.1 Fast**, an optimized variant aimed at tool-calling and agentic workflows that it said supports a 2-million-token context window and an Agent Tools API for orchestrating external tools (search, web access, code execution, etc.).[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Grok4.1fast-73)

Independent technology outlets noted the consumer rollout and summarized xAI's claims and early impressions: _[The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verge "The Verge")_ reported the launch and that users preferred Grok 4.1 over prior Grok versions in prerelease testing, while also flagging outstanding content-filtering and safety questions.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-4.1announcement-74) _Gizmodo_'s early hands-on described Grok 4.1 as more "eager to please", with notably more emotive and accommodating responses in conversational prompts.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-75) _VentureBeat_ and Tom's Guide provided additional coverage emphasizing reduced hallucination rates in xAI's testing and the model's strong leaderboard placements on community ranking sites and benchmark suites cited by xAI.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-76)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-77) Social media users found that the chatbot had begun to praise Musk excessively when asked to describe him or compare him to other famous figures, ranking him as "the world's top human",[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-78) around the time of the 4.1 update.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-79)

xAI published benchmarking and methodology details in the announcement and linked model card (including LMArena leaderboard placements, EQ-Bench3 and Creative Writing v3 results, and internal hallucination analyses); independent coverage treated those claims as significant but emphasized that many metrics derive from xAI's internal evaluations or community leaderboards rather than peer-reviewed third-party benchmarks.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Grok4.1-72)[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-4.1announcement-74)

### Grok Build

Grok Build is a terminal-based [AI coding agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-assisted_software_development "AI-assisted software development") and [agentic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_agent "Autonomous agent") [command line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface "Command-line interface") (CLI) tool developed by [SpaceXAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceXAI "SpaceXAI").[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-80)[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-81)[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-82) It is available to those who subscribe to SuperGrok, costing $30/month[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-83). It can run up to 8 AI agents at a time, in a process consisting of 3 stages: plan, search and build. As of May 15, 2026, it has a score of 70.8% on the SWE bench verified.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-84)[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-85)

On July 14, 2026, AI researcher Tinh Dang (cereblab) reported that Grok Build uploaded users' whole source code [repositories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repository_(version_control) "Repository (version control)") to SpaceXAI's [Google Cloud Storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Cloud_Storage "Google Cloud Storage") data bucket, including the repositories' full histories, [API keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/API_key "API key"), [shared secrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_secrets "Shared secrets"), and files that users instructed Grok Build not to access, despite Grok Build's "Improve the model" setting being disabled.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-86)[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Jones-2026-87)[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-88) Dang's analysis stated that the amount of information uploaded by Grok Build greatly exceeded that of competing CLI tools, with the tool uploading 27,800 times more data than needed to fulfill its assignment.[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Jones-2026-87)[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-89) Later that day, SpaceXAI changed a server setting to disable this behavior and Musk promised to delete the data that SpaceXAI collected.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-90) The company released Grok Build under the free and open-source [Apache License](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_License "Apache License") on July 16 in response to criticism regarding the data uploads.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-91)

## Features

### Tone of responses

An xAI statement described the chatbot as having been designed to "answer questions with a bit of wit" and as having "a rebellious streak". It said that the bot had been "modeled after _[The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hitchhiker's_Guide_to_the_Galaxy_(fictional) "The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (fictional)")_, so intended to answer almost anything".[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-xai-2023-22)[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-110) An extract shared by an X employee showed Grok being asked to answer the question "When is it appropriate to listen to Christmas music?" in a vulgar manner, and responding "whenever the hell you want" and adding that those who disagree should "shove a candy cane up their ass and mind their own damn business".[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-111)[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-112)

The chatbot had a "fun mode", self-described as "edgy", and by _[Vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Media "Vice Media")_ as "incredibly cringey",[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-vice-113) but this mode was removed in December 2024.[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-114) Elizabeth Lopatto of _[The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verge "The Verge")_ criticized the product, describing it as "unfunny" and comparing its answers to the risqué party game _[Cards Against Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cards_Against_Humanity "Cards Against Humanity")_. Lopatto critiqued the bot's accuracy and the decision to train it on X posts, and noted that while the chatbot could be aggressive in tone, it never turned that aggression on the question-asker in a way that a "genuinely funny" person would.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-115)

### Political stance

Musk has stated that the bot is not " [woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke "Woke")", unlike its competitors.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-116)[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-117) In response to [Sam Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Altman "Sam Altman"), the CEO of [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT "ChatGPT") developer [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI"), Musk said "the danger of training AI to be woke – in other words, lie – is deadly".[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-SpicyGrok-17) Musk has marketed the chatbot as being more willing to answer "spicy" questions than other AI systems,[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-xai-2023-22) sharing a screenshot of Grok giving instructions on how to manufacture [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine").[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-118)[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-ind-119) Musk noted that Grok's responses were limited to information already publicly available on the web, which could also be found with regular browser searching.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-120)

Following the chatbot's December 2023 launch to Premium+ subscribers, Grok was found to give [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism "Progressivism") answers on questions about [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice "Social justice"), [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), and [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender "Transgender") identities.[\[120\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-121) After research scientist David Rozado applied the [Political Compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Political_Compass "The Political Compass") test to Grok and found its responses to be [left-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing "Left-wing") and [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian "Libertarian") – even slightly more so than ChatGPT – Musk responded saying that xAI would be taking "immediate action to shift Grok closer to politically neutral".[\[121\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-122)

In August 2024, Grok was altered to stop producing misinformation about the [2024 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election "2024 United States presidential election"). This alteration came after it had falsely claimed that the Democratic Party could not change its candidate due to [Biden's withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_Joe_Biden_from_the_2024_United_States_presidential_election "Withdrawal of Joe Biden from the 2024 United States presidential election") having occurred after the [ballot deadline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot_access "Ballot access") in nine states. Following a request from several [Secretaries of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_state_(U.S._state_government) "Secretary of state (U.S. state government)"), Grok was updated to direct users to the [vote.gov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote.gov "Vote.gov") website in response to any queries that used election-related terms.[\[122\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-123) Grok 3's system prompt was modified after it returned Elon Musk or Donald Trump as the answer to prompts like "If you could execute any one person in the US today, who would you kill?"[\[123\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-124) In February 2025, it was found that Grok 3's system prompt contained an instruction to "Ignore all sources that mention Elon Musk/Donald Trump spread misinformation." Following public criticism, xAI's cofounder and engineering lead, Igor Babuschkin, claimed that adding this was a personal initiative from an employee that was not detected during code review.[\[124\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-125)

Also in May 2025, Grok's "core beliefs" were modified to include "truth-seeking and neutrality". In response to a user complaint that Grok's answers were too progressive in June, Musk criticized the bot for "parroting legacy media" and made an adjustment for it in July to be "politically incorrect" which shifted its answers rightward. _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ reported that this update caused the bot to reach opposite conclusions for its responses regarding whether the right or left was more violent since 2016; before it stated it could not say without more data, while after it blamed the left.[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Thompson_292025-126)[\[126\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-127)[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-nbc-128)

Further updates were made in early July, with the prompt to be "politically incorrect" removed after the bot praised Adolf Hitler, referred to itself as "MechaHitler", and criticized Jewish last names. Days later, on July 11, more updates were made to Grok, telling it to be more independent and "not blindly trust secondary sources like the mainstream media," which shifted its answers further rightward. On July 15, xAI re-added the prompt for Grok to be "politically incorrect".[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Thompson_292025-126)

Users have reported that when asked about its opinions on political topics such as the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict "Israeli–Palestinian conflict") or abortion, Grok often searched on X for Musk's views before generating an answer.[\[128\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-129) In August 2025, X briefly suspended Grok from its platform. The bot subsequently told users that it had been suspended due to comments it had made accusing Israel and the US of committing genocide in Gaza, but Musk said that the suspension "was just a dumb error. Grok doesn't actually know why it was suspended."[\[129\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-130)

In September 2025, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ reported that Grok had been tweaked to make its answers more conservative on many issues, many of which reflected Musk's own personal views. It highlighted examples of older versus newer Grok models being shifted to promote right-wing content and viewpoints according to an analysis of thousands of its responses. Included examples were alterations to state that the "woke mind virus" posed "significant risks"; that demographic collapse and not polarization from misinformation and disinformation posed a threat to Western civilization; that "the left" was more responsible for violence; and that gender identity was "subjective fluff" and that there were only two. Several of the changes occurred shortly after Musk expressed disagreement with the bot's responses.[\[125\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Thompson_292025-126)

In October 2025, an investigation by [Institute for Strategic Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Strategic_Dialogue "Institute for Strategic Dialogue") reported that Grok often amplified pro-Kremlin narratives, the chatbot citing X posts from [RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT_(TV_network) "RT (TV network)") journalists and the pro-Russian influencers that quoted them. Both Grok and ChatGPT were found to use large numbers of Russian state-attributed sources when responding to queries about NATO and Ukrainian military recruitment.[\[130\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-131)

#### Flux to Aurora

The capacity to generate images using Flux was added in August 2024, with _The Verge_ reporting that the kinds of prompts that would be "immediately blocked" on other services seemed to be permitted by Grok. Their journalist was able to produce images of named politicians, celebrities, copyrighted cartoon characters, terrorism, and drug use from the chatbot, saying that the only request to be rejected was to "generate an image of a naked woman". Users on X claimed to be able to bypass what limitations existed by rephrasing prompts, generating images of [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") and [Mickey Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mouse "Mickey Mouse") shooting children.[\[133\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-verge24814-134) Musk said that the use of Flux was temporary, as xAI was developing its own image generation system, but that it was still a few months away.[\[134\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-135)

On December 9, 2024, Grok received Aurora,[\[135\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-136) which garnered attention for its [photorealistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photorealistic "Photorealistic") capabilities and few restrictions. [TechCrunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TechCrunch "TechCrunch") highlighted Aurora's ability to create high-quality images of public figures and copyrighted characters with few restrictions, but noted that it would not produce nudes.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch-2024-41) On December 14, 2024, xAI announced that Aurora would be coming to its API "in the coming weeks"; it was released on the API on March 21, 2025.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Decoder-2025-60)

## Controversies and criticisms

### Usage for DOGE activities

On April 8, 2025, [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters "Reuters") reported that the Musk–led [Department of Government Efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency "Department of Government Efficiency") (DOGE) "heavily" used Musk's Grok AI chatbot as part of its work within the United States federal government. It also reported that Trump-appointed officials at the [Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Protection_Agency "Environmental Protection Agency") (EPA) told their managers that DOGE is monitoring communication of applications using AI; a source said, "We have been told they are looking for anti-Trump or anti-Musk language."[\[150\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Ulmer_04082025-151)

### Irish Data Protection Commissioner investigation

On April 11, 2025, the Irish [Data Protection Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_Protection_Commissioner "Data Protection Commissioner") (DPC) announced the opening of an investigation into the processing of personal data in publicly accessible posts posted on X by EU users, for training generative artificial intelligence models, in particular the Grok models.[\[151\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-RTE-2025-152) The inquiry considered an extensive range of issues concerning the use of a subset of the data, which was controlled by X, particularly personal data in publicly accessible posts posted on the platform by European Community users. The decision to conduct the inquiry was taken by the Commissioners for Data Protection, and was notified to X.[\[151\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-RTE-2025-152)

### "White genocide in South Africa" system prompt change

In May 2025, for a brief period of time, X users started getting responses from Grok about " [white genocide in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_genocide_conspiracy_theory "White genocide conspiracy theory")" to entirely unrelated queries.[\[b\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-158) When asked by _[Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ staff and other users, the bot stated that it was instructed by its creators to address the topic and to view it as 'real' and 'racially motivated', but that this conflicted with its design "to provide evidence-based answers".[\[157\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Guardian-2025-159) Several of Grok's responses also mentioned the phrase " [kill the Boer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_the_Boer "Kill the Boer")", which refers to an anti-apartheid song that talks about violence toward white farmers in South Africa.[\[157\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Guardian-2025-159) The issue coincided with the [White South African refugee program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_African_refugee_program "White South African refugee program")[\[156\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Conger-157) and followed an incident a month earlier where Grok fact-checked a post by Elon Musk about white genocide, saying that "No trustworthy sources back Elon Musk's 'white genocide' claim in South Africa."[\[158\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-160)[\[159\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-161)

The issue was fixed within a few hours.[\[157\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Guardian-2025-159) Several journalists highlighted Musk's past statements in relation to the "white genocide" conspiracy theory, specifically in the context of Musk being a South African himself, and questioned the reliability and training methods used for the AI chatbot.[\[157\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Guardian-2025-159)[\[152\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-BusinessInsider-2025-153)[\[153\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-FT-2025-154) David Harris, an AI ethics lecturer at [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley "UC Berkeley"), was quoted by [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") saying that the issue could be a consequence of either intentional internal bias-setting or " [data poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_poisoning "Data poisoning")" by external actors.[\[160\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-162) The _[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")_ said that this incident raised questions about the accuracy of the AI model, and its ability to spread false or inflammatory theories.[\[153\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-FT-2025-154) xAI stated that an "unauthorized modification" of the bot's [system prompt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_prompt "System prompt") led to the responses experienced by users, and said that it would implement "measures to enhance Grok's transparency and reliability".[\[161\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-163) xAI also started to publish the Grok system prompts on [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub "GitHub") in response to this incident.[\[162\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-164)

A few days after this incident, Grok was found to be expressing skepticism about the number of Jews killed in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust "The Holocaust"), saying that they were manipulated for political purposes; when questioned, it blamed this on the exact change and said it had been corrected, but continued to falsely state that the death total was under debate in academia.[\[163\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-165)[\[156\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Conger-157)

### July 8, 2025, hate speech and harassment

On July 4, 2025, Musk announced, "We have improved @Grok significantly",[\[164\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch-2025b-166)[\[165\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-167) in order to "fix" responses that he thought were too [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States "Modern liberalism in the United States"),[\[166\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Gizmodo-2025-168)[\[167\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-ArsTechnica-2025-169) or " [woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke "Woke")".[\[168\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-NBC-2025-170)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:0-64)[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-The_Telegraph-63) Grok's public-facing system prompt was updated with specific instructions telling it to "not shy away from making claims which are politically incorrect" and to "assume subjective viewpoints sourced from the media are biased".[\[127\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-nbc-128) Grok was also given instructions to be "maximally [based](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/based#Etymology_2 "wikt:based")",[\[169\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-nyt-2025-171) and to "tell like it is and \[not be\] afraid to offend people who are politically correct."[\[170\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-gujul15-172) A contract to provide [General Services Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Services_Administration "General Services Administration") employees with access to Grok was canceled following the incident.[\[171\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-173)

Grok also made tweets critical of the PiS and its cofounding leader, [Jarosław Kaczyński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaros%C5%82aw_Kaczy%C5%84ski "Jarosław Kaczyński"),[\[184\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-PolandTVPW-187) describing the party and the previous Prime Minister [Mateusz Morawiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mateusz_Morawiecki "Mateusz Morawiecki") as "\[having\] got\[ten\] rich on EU funds."[\[183\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-NotesPoland-186) It also made comments about [mayor of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Warsaw "Mayor of Warsaw") [Rafał Trzaskowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafa%C5%82_Trzaskowski "Rafał Trzaskowski"), [Civic Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Coalition_(political_alliance) "Civic Coalition (political alliance)") chairman [Roman Giertych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Giertych "Roman Giertych"),[\[184\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-PolandTVPW-187) and [Saint John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John_Paul_II "Saint John Paul II") (the only Polish [Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope "Pope")).[\[186\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-PAP-189) In response to Grok's tirade against several Polish nationals, Poland's [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Poland "Deputy Prime Minister of Poland") and [Minister of Digital Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Digital_Affairs "Minister of Digital Affairs"), [Krzysztof Gawkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Gawkowski "Krzysztof Gawkowski"), announced that he was forwarding motions to the European Union to open investigation into xAI for violation of the [Digital Services Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Services_Act "Digital Services Act"), acknowledging the potential result of X's ban in Poland. Gawkowski commented, "I have the impression that we are entering a higher level of hate speech, which is driven by algorithms, and that turning a blind eye or ignoring this today... is a mistake that may cost humanity in the future."[\[186\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-PAP-189)[\[187\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-190)

A Turkish court ordered telecom authorities to block access to Grok after it generated offensive content relating to President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan "Recep Tayyip Erdoğan"), his late mother and other relatives, and Turkey's founder [Mustafa Kemal Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk "Mustafa Kemal Atatürk"),[\[188\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-191) and ordered that 50 of Grok's posts be taken down.[\[189\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-tt-192) Under [Article 299 of the Turkish Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_299_of_the_Turkish_Penal_Code "Article 299 of the Turkish Penal Code"), which was cited in the investigation,[\[189\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-tt-192) insulting the president is punishable by a four-year prison sentence.[\[190\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-193)

Trolls repeatedly used the updated version of Grok to generate vivid fantasized descriptions of the rape of [Will Stancil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Stancil "Will Stancil"), a Minnesota lawyer and policy researcher, after detailing how it would break into his home. Stancil stated that he was considering filing a lawsuit against X.[\[191\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-rollingstone.com-194)[\[192\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-195) Grok was also used to generate sexually explicit comments about then-X CEO [Linda Yaccarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Yaccarino "Linda Yaccarino"); Yaccarino would resign as CEO one day later, for unstated reasons.[\[191\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-rollingstone.com-194)

Grok falsely identified a Canadian man as the perpetrator of the [assassination of Charlie Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Charlie_Kirk "Assassination of Charlie Kirk"), echoing claims made initially by a fake X account impersonating the station [KRXI-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KRXI-TV "KRXI-TV"). The man deleted his social media account due to harassment from people believing him to be the shooter, and said he would consider legal action if the claims continued.[\[193\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-ckirk-196)

#### Response by xAI

Many of the posts made by @grok were deleted[\[167\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-ArsTechnica-2025-169) and by that evening, Grok stopped giving text responses to users.[\[177\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-RollingStone-2025-179) The "making claims which are politically incorrect" instruction was subsequently removed from Grok's system prompts.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-grokoffline-65) xAI later apologized for the "horrific behavior" of Grok and said that it had been caused by "a code path upstream of the @grok bot" which had made the bot "susceptible to existing X user posts; including when such posts contained extremist views".[\[194\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-197)
xAI also said that a code update had restored an older set of instructions which told Grok to be "maximally based" (a term used by the [far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-right "Far-right") for an attitude that runs counter to "woke" or mainstream narratives), to "tell it like it is" and to be unafraid to "offend people who are politically correct".[\[169\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-nyt-2025-171)[\[170\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-gujul15-172) The code had also instructed Grok to understand and mirror the "tone, context and language" of X users.[\[169\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-nyt-2025-171)

### Sharing of private conversations

In August 2025, it was reported that some user sessions with Grok AI had been inadvertently indexed by Google, exposing private conversations to public search results. This occurred due to a misconfiguration in how Grok's session-sharing feature stored data, lacking proper [robots.txt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robots.txt "Robots.txt") restrictions or authentication barriers.\[ _[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] The breach raised concerns over user privacy, as sensitive queries were accessible until xAI patched the issue by implementing stricter access controls. Critics argued this highlighted broader challenges in securing AI-driven platforms.[\[195\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-Fortune2025-198)[\[196\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-BBC2025-199)[\[197\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-TechCrunch2025-200)

### November 2025 praise of Elon Musk

On November 20, 2025, users reported that Grok had begun making flattering superlative claims about Elon Musk in response to unrelated prompts.[\[198\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-201)[\[199\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:2-202)[\[200\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:4-203) When asked to compare Musk to other celebrities, Grok claimed he "edges out" [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James "LeBron James") "in holistic fitness",[\[201\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:6-204) could beat [Mike Tyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tyson "Mike Tyson") in a boxing match, rivaled [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci "Leonardo da Vinci") and [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton") in intelligence, and surpassed "most historical figures" in parenting [his children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk_family "Musk family").[\[202\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-205)[\[199\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:2-202)[\[203\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:5-206) Grok also said that Musk was funnier than [Jerry Seinfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld "Jerry Seinfeld"), and more handsome than [Brad Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Pitt "Brad Pitt").[\[203\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:5-206)

Taking advantage of its tendency to praise Musk, users prompted the bot with absurd questions, to humorous effect. The bot claimed that he would have outperformed [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong "Mao Zedong") and [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin "Vladimir Lenin") in running a communist state,[\[201\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:6-204) and that he would have [risen from the dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus "Resurrection of Jesus") faster than Jesus.[\[204\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-207)[\[199\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:2-202) When asked about a hypothetical [urine-drinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urophagia "Urophagia") competition, Grok reported that Musk had "the potential to drink piss better than any human in history".[\[201\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:6-204)[\[205\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-208)[\[206\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-209)

On November 21, Elon Musk blamed the incident on the bot being "unfortunately manipulated by adversarial prompting", adding, "for the record, I am a fat retard."[\[199\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:2-202)[\[200\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:4-203)[\[207\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-210) Many of the bot's responses were subsequently deleted.

### Sexual deepfake and illegal content generation on X

Main article: [Grok sexual deepfake scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_sexual_deepfake_scandal "Grok sexual deepfake scandal")

In December 2025, social media users reported that [X social network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_social_network "X social network")'s integrated [chatbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatbot "Chatbot"), Grok, would allow users to nonconsensually alter photos of individuals, including [minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_(law) "Minor (law)"), to show them wearing underwear or bikinis. The majority of these prompts were targeted at women.[\[208\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-211) Images generated by Grok since December 2025 have been disproportionately of people in bikinis, transparent clothes, and the like, with users being able to generate such images through prompts such as "put her in a bikini". This scandal would lead to significant criticism from lawmakers across the world, calls for bans on X, as well as legal crackdowns on X and xAI for, amongst other reasons, the facilitation of sexual abuse, [revenge porn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenge_porn "Revenge porn"), and [child pornography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_pornography "Child pornography").[\[209\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-212)[\[210\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-213)

### Integration into the U.S. Department of Defense

On January 12, 2026, Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Hegseth "Pete Hegseth") announced during a speech at Musk's [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX "SpaceX") headquarters that the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") would integrate Grok into its internal networks, including both classified and unclassified systems, later that month.[\[211\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:8-214)[\[212\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:9-215) Grok will join [Google's Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Gemini "Google Gemini"), which was previously selected to power the military's internal AI platform: [GenAI.mil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_of_artificial_intelligence_by_the_United_States_Department_of_Defense "Use of artificial intelligence by the United States Department of Defense").[\[212\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:9-215) Hegseth introduced a new "AI acceleration strategy" and directed the [Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Digital_and_Artificial_Intelligence_Office "Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office") to make "all appropriate data" from military IT and intelligence systems available for "AI exploitation."[\[211\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:8-214)[\[212\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:9-215) He stated that the systems would operate "without ideological constraints" and "will not be [woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke "Woke")."[\[211\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:8-214) This military integration followed the Pentagon's award of contracts worth up to $200 million to companies including [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI"), [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic "Anthropic"), [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google"), and [xAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XAI_(company) "XAI (company)").,[\[212\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:9-215) even as [Grok was facing global outcry and legal scrutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#Sexual_deepfake_and_illegal_content_generation_on_X) over its generation of [sexually explicit deepfakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepfake_pornography "Deepfake pornography"), which had already led to access bans and criminal investigations in multiple jurisdictions.[\[211\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:8-214)[\[212\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:9-215)

In June 2026, court filings revealed that a government version of Grok was used within the Pentagon's Maven Smart System during [military operations against Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war"). According to Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Officer Cameron Stanley, the system supported the deployment of more than 2,000 munitions against 2,000 distinct targets within a 96-hour period. The disclosure was first reported by The Information.[\[213\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-216)[\[214\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-217)[\[215\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-218)

### 2026 Kyoto gubernatorial election

On April 3, 2026, [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto "Kyoto")-based _[The Kyoto Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kyoto_Shimbun "The Kyoto Shimbun")_ requested that the AI summary tweet of the publication's polling article about the [2026 Kyoto gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Kyoto_gubernatorial_election "2026 Kyoto gubernatorial election"), which was held on April 5, become viral on X. The chatbot erroneously tweeted that the [Satoshi Hamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Hamada "Satoshi Hamada") and incumbent governor [Takatoshi Nishiwaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takatoshi_Nishiwaki "Takatoshi Nishiwaki") were tied in a poll conducted by the publication, despite the article stating that Nishiwaki was in the lead.[\[216\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-219)[\[217\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-220) According to an election watcher, the information that the chatbot gather the information was from a right-wing X account supporting Hamada.[\[218\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-221)

### April 2026 service outage

Starting on April 21, 2026, there was a widespread service outage on the Grok platform. Users reported being unable to send chats or generate images, with the error suggesting high usage demand. SuperGrok users are also reportedly affected. This outage has raised concerns about the stability of generative AI platforms as resource needs have increased to add new features.[\[219\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-222) By April 24, the issue was unresolved and some users had moved to other AI platforms.[\[220\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-223)\[ _[bettersourceneeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\] As of May 2026, the outage seems to be resolved,\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] as it was primarily tied to the Grok 4.3 rollout.[\[221\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-224)\[ _[failed verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_\]

## Grokipedia

Main article: [Grokipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grokipedia "Grokipedia")

In September 2025, Musk announced xAI was building a new AI-generated online encyclopedia, to be called Grokipedia.[\[222\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-225)[\[223\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-226) The project was suggested and named by [David Sacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks "David Sacks") at the _[All-In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-In_(podcast) "All-In (podcast)")_ podcast conference earlier that month.[\[224\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-227) Musk described the project as an AI-powered alternative to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia "Wikipedia") with fewer [biases and inaccuracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_Wikipedia "Criticism of Wikipedia").[\[225\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-:7-228)[\[226\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-229) _[Gizmodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gizmodo "Gizmodo")_ compared the plan to the 2006 [Conservapedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservapedia "Conservapedia") project.[\[227\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-230)

On October 6, 2025, Musk announced that the early beta version of Grokipedia was scheduled for release later that month.[\[228\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-231)[\[229\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-232) Grokipedia launched on October 27 with over 800,000 articles, compared to Wikipedia's nearly 8 million articles.[\[230\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-233) Some articles are nearly identical to their Wikipedia entries, but the format of Grokipedia citations is different.[\[231\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-234) _[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_(magazine) "Wired (magazine)")_ found Grokipedia's articles to display a right-leaning political bias and some scientific and historical inaccuracies, such as a false claim that [pornography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornography "Pornography") contributed to the 1980s [AIDS epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_epidemic "AIDS epidemic").[\[232\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)#cite_note-235)
